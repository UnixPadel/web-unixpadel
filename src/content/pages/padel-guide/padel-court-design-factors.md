A padel court looks simple: a steel frame, glass, mesh, turf and lights. In practice it behaves like a small building, exposed to wind, rain and heavy daily use. Projects that run smoothly for years have one thing in common: the key decisions were taken in the right order, before anyone compared quotes.

These are the five factors our engineers review on every project.

## 1. Site, footprint and foundation

Everything starts with the plot. Check the usable area against the court's needs: the 20 x 10 m playing area, a base of about 20.8 x 10.8 m for the structure, access openings, lighting posts and paths. If out-of-court play is allowed, add the safety areas beside the openings. The exact figures are in [padel court dimensions](/padel-guide/padel-court-dimensions/).

Then look at the ground. A conventional court needs a level slab (within 3 mm over 3 m) with a reinforced perimeter beam for the anchors and a drainage plan: a 1% slope on smooth concrete, or porous concrete that drains through itself. Our [foundation guide](/padel-court-foundation/) compares concrete, porous concrete and asphalt bases.

Where excavation is impossible or too expensive (rooftops, car parks, leased land) the [Flat Floor court](/innovative-courts/flat-floor-padel-court/) stands on a pedestal system over any flat, stable surface, without a concrete slab.

## 2. Structure and wind load

A padel court carries large glass panels on a steel frame, so wind is the main structural load. The frame must stay rigid enough to keep the glass from vibrating and the anchors from loosening.

What to ask for:

- **Steel grade and profiles:** S355 structural steel; on Unixpadel standard courts, 100 x 50 x 2 mm columns and 100 x 50 x 3 mm beams.
- **Corrosion protection:** hot-dip galvanizing to ISO 1461 followed by electrostatic powder coating. In coastal or humid areas see [coastal padel courts](/padel-guide/coastal-padel-courts-construction/).
- **Wind rating for your site:** the [Standard court](/standard-padel-court/) withstands winds of 120–140 km/h with its three-pillar corners; the [WP Challenge court](/wp-challenge-padel-court/) adds corner reinforcement for very exposed sites.
- **A static calculation:** Unixpadel courts are supplied with static analysis approved by Istanbul Technical University (ITU).

The [steel types guide](/padel-guide/steel-types-for-padel-courts/) explains why galvanized structural steel is the standard choice.

![3D render of a panoramic padel court frame with glass walls and mesh on a concrete base](/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/WhatsApp-Image-2026-01-09-at-14.53.34.jpeg)

## 3. Glass walls and mesh

The walls are part of the game, so they must give the same rebound everywhere and stay safe when players hit them.

- **Glass:** tempered safety glass to EN 12150-1, 10 mm on standard courts and 12 mm on [panoramic courts](/panoramic-padel-court/), fixed with rubber gaskets so glass never touches steel.
- **Mesh:** electro-welded steel mesh within the FIP limits for opening size and wire thickness, flush with the glass and with all weld points protected.
- **Layout:** stepped or straight side walls, both allowed by the FIP.

Details and quality checks are in the [net and mesh quality guide](/padel-guide/padel-net-and-mesh-systems-quality-guide/).

## 4. Playing surface and lighting

The surface sets the speed of the game and how players' joints feel after a session. Most clubs use sand-filled artificial turf; the FIP also accepts concrete and synthetic surfaces. Compare the options in [padel court surfaces](/padel-guide/padel-court-surface/) and browse Unixpadel [turf systems](/turfs/).

Lighting decides whether the court earns money in the evening. Plan LED floodlights that deliver an even 300–500 lux on club courts, mounted outside the playing area at least 6 m high, and aimed so they do not reflect into players' eyes from the glass. Courts used for television need around 1,000 lux of vertical light.

## 5. Climate and canopy

Rain, strong sun and snow are the main reasons outdoor courts sit empty. In wet or very hot climates a canopy often pays for itself through steady bookings and longer turf life.

Options range from fixed membrane or sandwich-panel roofs to retractable systems; see Unixpadel [canopies](/canopy/) and [innovative canopies](/innovative-canopy/). Indoors and under a roof, keep the clear height at 6 m minimum and preferably 8 m, as explained in [ideal ceiling height](/padel-guide/ideal-ceiling-height-padel-court/).
